1 in 6 Malaysian adults lives with diabetes.

6 tsp of added sugar a day is the WHO's suggested limit.

living with diabetes
isn't all or nothing.

Both of our parents live with diabetes, so we've seen how hard this balance is. Most people end up at one of two extremes.

extreme one

carry on as usual.

Giving up sweet things feels impossible, so nothing changes. The daily teh, the kuih, the desserts all stay, and so does the sugar.

extreme two

give it all up.

No kuih at Raya, no sweet drink with friends. It's hard to keep up, and food stops being something to enjoy.

the middle way

keep the taste, change the sugar.

Keep the drinks and recipes you love and change what sweetens them. Small swaps you can actually live with, every day.

tebu is a sweetener, not a medicine or treatment. If you're managing diabetes or another health condition, talk to your doctor or dietitian about what's right for you.

small swaps,
not sacrifices.

Order it kosong.

Ask for your teh or kopi without sugar, then add a drop or two of tebu yourself. You decide how sweet.

02

Swap just one a day.

One teh tarik a day is about 7 kg of sugar a year. Swapping only that one makes a difference.

03

Keep the food you love.

Kuih, halwa, sambal. Keep the recipes, change the sweetener.

    Our story

    why did we rethink sweetness?

    It started at home, with our parents.

    Both of our parents live with diabetes, so sugar became something we had to count at every meal. Every alternative we tried tasted bitter or metallic.

    The game changer, coming from an Indian household, was halwa: a rich, fudge-like Indian sweet slow-cooked with ghee. One pot for a family of five usually takes about a cup of sugar, around 200 g. Made with tebu instead, it caramelised and tasted exactly the way it always had.

    That's why we made tebu: so the people we love never have to give up sweetness.
